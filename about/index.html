About

Octavian Banica

I build backend systems where the output has to be both fast and trustworthy — fixed-income analytics, custom wireless hardware, DSP telephony. I've spent 35 years moving between very different kinds of hardware and one very consistent kind of problem.

How I got here

The career doesn't look like a straight line if you read it as a list: embedded ADSL and digital-audio-broadcast software, medical-imaging automation, a cement factory's industrial control system, Fax-over-IP on DSP hardware, cryptography and packet classification for high-throughput wireless broadband, and — for the last decade — fixed-income analytics at Bloomberg.

Looking back at it, though, there's a pattern. Almost every one of those projects put me at the point where a system's output had to be trusted, not just produced — where "it usually works" isn't good enough, because the thing downstream is a wireless subscriber's connection, a fax handshake, or a number that a financial analyst is about to act on. I keep ending up doing the validation, the reconciliation, the diagnostic tooling — the unglamorous work that makes the rest of the system defensible. That's an interpretation of my own history, not a mission statement I set out with, but it's held up consistently enough across three decades that I trust it now.

Right now

I left Bloomberg in February 2026, after eleven years owning the architecture of its fixed-income analytics platform. I'm currently building Logic Soft Design, an independent software consulting and development practice, and I'm also open to conversations about the next senior systems engineering role.
